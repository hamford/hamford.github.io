Turn Sequence
Battle Round Overview
5 phases in each player turn
▾

A battle round has two turns, one for each player. Each turn follows this order:

  1. 1. Command: Gain CP, resolve Command‑phase rules, take Battle‑shock tests, and determine objective control.
  2. 2. Movement: Each unit either moves normally, Advances or stays still; units in melee can Fall Back instead.
  3. 3. Shooting: Eligible units shoot one at a time, picking targets and resolving ranged attacks.
  4. 4. Charge: Units declare and roll charges to enter Engagement Range.
  5. 5. Fight: Units in melee fight, with Fights First units going before the rest.

Always finish a full phase for one player before moving on to the next.

Command Phase
1. Command Points & Rules
CP and phase‑timed abilities
▾
  • Gain CP: At the start of each player’s Command phase, both players gain 1CP.
  • Command‑phase rules: Resolve all abilities that say they happen “in your Command phase” now (buffs, healing, etc.).
2. Battle‑shock
Morale & objective control
▾

Units that are Below Half‑strength must test for Battle‑shock.

  • Who tests: Any unit that has lost more than half its starting models, or half its wounds if it is a single‑model unit.
  • Test: Roll 2D6; if the result is equal to or above the unit’s Leadership, it passes. Otherwise, it is Battle‑shocked until your next Command phase.

Battle‑shocked units

  • OC becomes 0, so they cannot control objectives while the shock lasts.
  • You cannot use Stratagems on them.
  • If they Fall Back, they must take Desperate Escape tests.
3. Controlling Objectives
Who holds the marker?
▾
  • Range: A model is in range if it is within 3" horizontally and 5" vertically of the marker.
  • Level of Control: Sum the OC values of all your models in range; do the same for your opponent.
  • Who controls: The player with the higher total OC controls that objective; equal totals mean it’s contested.
Movement Phase
1. Moving Units
Normal, Advance, Fall Back, Stationary
▾

Movement options

  • Normal Move: Move each model up to its M value, staying out of Engagement Range (1") of enemies.
  • Advance: Move up to M + D6". You cannot enter Engagement Range, shoot, or charge this turn unless a rule allows it (e.g. [ASSAULT] weapons).
  • Remain Stationary: The unit does not move; useful for Heavy weapons and some abilities.
  • Fall Back: Units in Engagement Range move up to M" to leave melee; they normally cannot shoot or charge this turn.

Desperate Escape (when Falling Back)

  • Models that move through enemy models (unless they are TITANIC/FLY) or are Battle‑shocked must roll a D6; on 1–2, one model from the unit is destroyed.

Terrain & Flying

  • Low terrain (≤2"): Move over it normally.
  • Higher terrain: You must measure movement up and down when climbing over.
  • FLY: Units with FLY can move over enemy models and terrain when making Normal, Advance or Fall Back moves; distance is measured through the air.
Transports
Embark, disembark, and destroyed transports
▾

Embarking

  • To embark, every model in the unit must end a Normal, Advance or Fall Back move within 3" of a friendly Transport.
  • You cannot embark and disembark in the same Movement phase.

Disembarking

  • Only units that started the phase embarked can disembark, and only if the Transport has not Advanced or Fallen Back.
  • When disembarking, set the unit up wholly within 3" of the Transport and not within Engagement Range of enemies.
  • If the unit disembarks before the Transport moves, it can act normally. If it disembarks after the Transport moves, it cannot move or charge this turn but can otherwise act normally.

When a Transport is destroyed

  • Each embarked model rolls a D6 and may suffer a mortal wound depending on how far it is placed from the wreck; surviving models disembark and the unit is Battle‑shocked.
Reinforcements & Reserves
Bringing units in later
▾

Reserves

  • Units can start the game off the battlefield instead of being deployed normally.
  • When they arrive, they must be set up according to their rules, measuring distances horizontally from enemy units.
  • They count as having made a Normal Move in the turn they are set up.
  • Any Reserve unit not on the battlefield by the end of the game counts as destroyed.

Strategic Reserves

  • Only up to 25% of your army’s points can be put into Strategic Reserves, and they cannot include Fortifications.
  • From battle round 2, they can arrive from the table edges, following the distance and deployment restrictions.
  • They cannot be set up within 9" of enemy models.
Shooting Phase
Shooting Sequence
Who shoots, at what, and how
▾

Resolve shooting one unit at a time:

  1. 1. Pick a unit: It must be eligible to shoot based on its movement; units in Engagement Range usually cannot shoot, except Pistols or some MONSTER/VEHICLE rules (“Big Guns Never Tire”).
  2. 2. Pick targets: Choose targets for every weapon before rolling any dice. Weapons cannot split shots between different units, but a model with multiple weapons can choose different targets for each.
  3. 3. Make ranged attacks:
    • Roll to hit: 1D6 per attack, aiming to equal or beat BS.
    • Roll to wound: 1D6 per successful hit, using the Strength vs Toughness table.
    • Critical hits/wounds: unmodified 6s always succeed; 1s always fail. Hit/wound modifiers are capped at ±1.
  4. 4. Allocate attacks: The defender chooses which model in the target unit takes each attack, but must continue allocating to a model that already has lost wounds this phase until it is destroyed.
  5. 5. Saving throws: Roll 1D6 per allocated wound, modified by AP; if the result is less than the save needed, damage goes through. Invulnerable saves ignore AP.
  6. 6. Deal damage: Subtract Damage from the model’s wounds; excess damage is lost when it dies. Mortal wounds are resolved after normal damage and ignore saves.

You can “fast roll” similar weapons: roll all hits and wounds together for one profile against one target.

Cover & Terrain
Benefit of Cover
When you get +1 to saves
▾

Benefit of Cover (BoC)

  • BoC is typically +1 to armour saves against ranged attacks.
  • It doesn’t help models with a 3+ save or better against AP 0 attacks.
  • Multiple sources of BoC don’t stack.

Common terrain types (summary)

  • Craters / Rubble: Normal movement and visibility; BoC if the unit is wholly within.
  • Hills: Normal movement and visibility; BoC if the target isn’t fully visible to every model in the attacking unit.
  • Battlefield Debris: Can move over but not end on top; BoC if not fully visible to all attackers.
  • Woods: Harder to draw full visibility; BoC if wholly within or not fully visible to all firing models.
  • Ruins: Infantry/beasts/FLY move through; usually block line of sight; BoC if wholly inside or not fully visible.
  • Barricades / Fuel Pipes: Obstacle rules; give BoC if within 3" and not fully visible; special melee interaction for charging over them.
Charge Phase
Charge Sequence
Declaring and resolving charges
▾
  1. 1. Declare charges: Pick eligible units and declare which enemy units they will charge; targets must be within 12", line of sight is not strictly required.
  2. 2. Roll charge distance: Roll 2D6; this is the maximum distance the unit can move for the charge.
  3. 3. Check success: The charge fails if the unit cannot both reach Engagement Range of every declared target and stay in coherency. Otherwise it succeeds.
  4. 4. Move chargers: Move each model up to the rolled distance, not entering Engagement Range of units that were not declared targets, and trying to make base‑to‑base contact where possible.

Units that successfully charge gain a Fights First‑type bonus until the end of the turn.

Fight Phase
Fight Sequence
Pile‑in, attacks, consolidate
▾

Order of fighting

  • Start with the player whose turn it is not; players alternate choosing units that are eligible to fight.
  • Units with Fights First resolve their combats ahead of other eligible units.

Steps to fight

  1. 1. Pile‑in: Move each model up to 3" towards the nearest enemy model, staying in coherency and within Engagement Range if possible.
  2. 2. Choose targets: A model can fight enemies it is in Engagement Range of, or via friendly models in base contact; choose targets for all attacks first.
  3. 3. Make melee attacks: Resolve attacks like shooting, but using WS and melee weapon profiles.
  4. 4. Consolidate: Move up to 3" again, towards the closest enemy or onto objectives, while keeping coherency and Engagement conditions.
Universal Special Rules (USRs)
Common USRs
A few key ones at a glance
▾
  • ANTI‑KEYWORD X+ – Against the named target type, a wound roll of X+ counts as a Critical Wound.
  • ASSAULT – Can shoot after Advancing, but only with Assault weapons.
  • BLAST – Extra attacks vs big units; cannot shoot into combats involving your own units.
  • DEADLY DEMISE X – On destruction, on a 6 each nearby unit suffers X mortal wounds.
  • DEEP STRIKE – Deploys from Reserves, usually 9"+ from enemies.
  • DEVASTATING WOUNDS – On a Critical Wound, normal damage is turned into mortal wounds.
  • FEEL NO PAIN X+ – When losing a wound, roll D6; on X+ that wound is ignored.
  • FLY – Can move over models/terrain when moving, Advancing, Falling Back, or Charging.
  • HEAVY – If the unit Remained Stationary, add 1 to Hit rolls.
  • IGNORES COVER – Target cannot benefit from the Benefit of Cover against this attack.
  • INDIRECT FIRE – Can shoot targets out of line of sight but with a hit penalty and giving them BoC.
  • LEADER – Characters attach to Bodyguard units; you cannot allocate attacks to the Character while bodyguards remain.
  • LETHAL HITS – Critical Hits automatically wound.
  • MELTA X – At half range, add X to the Damage.
  • ONE SHOT – Weapon can be fired only once per battle.
  • PISTOL – Can shoot in Engagement Range, but generally not together with non‑Pistol weapons (unless Monster/Vehicle).
  • SCOUT X – Pre‑game move of X" after deployment, staying more than 9" from enemies; some Transports inherit this if they’re full of Scout units.
  • STEALTH – Enemies are −1 to hit the unit with ranged attacks when every model has Stealth.
  • SUSTAINED HITS X – Critical Hits generate X additional hits.
  • TORRENT – Attacks automatically hit the target.
  • TWIN‑LINKED – Re‑roll Wound rolls.
Core Stratagems (summary)
Re‑rolls & Morale
Command Re‑roll, Insane Bravery
▾
  • Command Re‑roll (1CP): Re‑roll a single roll (hit, wound, save, damage, Advance, Charge, Desperate Escape, Hazardous, number of attacks).
  • Insane Bravery (1CP): After failing a Battle‑shock test, treat that test as passed and the unit is not Battle‑shocked.
Movement & Position
Rapid Ingress, Heroic Interventions, Tank Shock
▾
  • Rapid Ingress (1CP): At the end of your opponent’s Movement phase, bring in one unit from Reserves as if it were your Reinforcements step (obeying normal timing limits).
  • Heroic Intervention (2CP): When an enemy finishes a charge, select a nearby unit to immediately declare and resolve a charge against that enemy, without gaining charge bonuses.
  • Tank Shock (1CP): When a VEHICLE charges, roll dice based on weapon Strength to inflict mortal wounds on the charged unit, up to a cap.
Defence & Reactions
Overwatch, Smokescreen, Go to Ground
▾
  • Fire Overwatch (1CP): React to enemy movement/charges by shooting them as if in your Shooting phase; only unmodified 6s to hit can score hits, and once per turn.
  • Smokescreen (1CP): Give a SMOKE unit Benefit of Cover and Stealth after it is selected as a shooting target.
  • Go to Ground (1CP): An INFANTRY unit gains a 6+ invulnerable save and Benefit of Cover when targeted by shooting.
Fight Phase Tricks
Counter‑offensive, Epic Challenge
▾
  • Counter‑offensive (2CP): After an enemy fights, immediately pick your unit in that combat to fight next, interrupting the normal order.
  • Epic Challenge (1CP): Give a CHARACTER’s melee attacks a precision‑style ability for the phase, letting them pick out Character models in Attached units.
Reference: Aircraft, Coherency, Visibility
Aircraft (Summary)
Movement and who can fight them
▾
  • Aircraft usually start in Reserves unless using Hover mode.
  • They move a minimum distance in a straight line and can pivot once; leaving the table often puts them into Strategic Reserves.
  • Only units with FLY can charge and fight Aircraft; Aircraft themselves cannot charge, pile in, or consolidate, and only fight units with FLY.
  • Other units can move underneath Aircraft but cannot end within Engagement Range of enemy Aircraft unless allowed.
Coherency, Engagement & Visibility
Basic distances to remember
▾

Unit Coherency

  • Units of 2–6 models: each model must be within 2" horizontally and 5" vertically of at least one other model in its unit.
  • Units of 7+ models: each model must be within that distance of at least two other models in the unit.
  • At the end of each turn, remove models until the unit is back in coherency.

Engagement Range

  • Within 1" horizontally and 5" vertically of an enemy model.
  • You cannot set up or end a Normal, Advance or Fall Back move in Engagement Range of enemy models.

Determining Visibility

  • A model is visible if you can see any part of it.
  • A unit is visible if at least one of its models is visible.
  • “Fully visible” means every facing part of a model can be seen; some terrain rules key off full visibility.

Re‑rolls & Modifiers

  • Unmodified rolls are the result after re‑rolls but before modifiers.
  • You can never re‑roll the same dice more than once.
  • If several dice are added together (e.g. 2D6), you must re‑roll all of them.
  • Hit, wound and save modifiers are usually capped at ±1.
